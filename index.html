About

I am a mathematician who likes problems with real consequences, the kind where the equation has to agree with a patient, a courtroom or a classroom.

My doctoral work was on analytical and numerical solutions of nonlinear parabolic partial differential equations. That training still shapes everything I do. Over the years it has carried me into computational neuroscience, convection in micropolar fluids, optimisation for cardiac care, and, more recently, digital forensics, explainable AI and digital twins for healthcare.

I began my teaching career as a lecturer at Maharani’s Science College, Bengaluru. From 1997 to 2019 I was at Mount Carmel College, Autonomous, where I coordinated the postgraduate programme and later headed the Mathematics Department. I then taught at CHRIST (Deemed to be University), Lavasa campus, and at St Francis College, and served as founding Principal of MVJ Degree College. Since 2024 I have been at JAIN (Deemed-to-be University), teaching BTech and MTech students and working with the Cyber Security and Digital Twin Technologies Research Centre.

I still enjoy a first-year calculus class as much as a research seminar.

Research

Areas of work

Six threads, one habit: write down the model, check it against data, and explain the result in terms people can act on.

∂

Mathematical modelling

Nonlinear PDEs, fractional-order diffusion and stability analysis, from calcium signalling to double-diffusive convection.

σ

Digital forensics

Detecting manipulated images, audio and video, tracing provenance, and making that evidence hold up under scrutiny.

ξ

Explainable AI

Models whose decisions can be inspected and defended, applied to forensics, agriculture and medicine.

Φ

Digital twins in health

Physics-informed, fractional-order twins for palliative care and for women's health through the menopausal years.

λ

Multimodal signal processing

Combining audio, video and physiological signals, including multi-video audio forgery detection.

ψ

Quantum computing

Teaching and course design that takes learners from linear algebra to working quantum circuits.
